- (direction) Input brief: beginners blocked by jargon, choice paralysis, fear of mistakes; advisors don't scale, robo-advisors don't teach. 6 agents: Finance Q&A, Portfolio Analysis, Market Analysis, Goal Planning, News Synthesizer, Tax Education. LangGraph router, RAG over 50-100 articles, education-not-advice disclaimers. Original stack: LangGraph/LangChain/OpenAI/FAISS/all-MiniLM-L6-v2/Streamlit/async Python. APIs: OpenAI, Alpha Vantage (25 req/day, 5/min), SerpAPI (100/mo) or Tavily (1000/mo).
- (direction) Known risk from brief: Alpha Vantage 25/day cap is #1 demo killer; need aggressive 30-min TTL caching + mock data fallbacks early.
- (direction) User wants Next.js + FastAPI in one monorepo deployed on Vercel instead of Streamlit.
- (insight) Constraint to brainstorm against: Vercel Python serverless ~250MB unzipped function limit (sentence-transformers/PyTorch + FAISS exceed it), no persistent in-memory cache across invocations (TTL cache needs external KV), function duration limits affect long LangGraph runs/streaming.
- (decision) Stance: Facilitator. Techniques: Five Whys, Six Thinking Hats, Mind Mapping, Scenario Cross (random pick), + coach-chosen Chaos Engineering (Wild) — fits demo-killer/deployment-risk theme.
- (technique) started Five Whys
- (insight) Why #1: they closed the tab because they fear losing the money by investing it 'the wrong way'.
- (insight) Why #2: belief in a money-losing 'wrong way' comes from a family member who lost money investing — inherited, secondhand fear.
- (insight) Why #3: they can't tell their situation apart from the relative's loss because they don't understand the jargon — can't diagnose what went wrong for the relative.
- (insight) Why #4: googling doesn't fix it — definitions don't create comfort; they believe investing requires 'finance experience' they don't have (identity/permission gap, not information gap).
- (question) Why #5 (answered as the user's own question): How can a beginner go slow and invest in something not risky that still beats a savings account?
- (insight) Root cause (Five Whys): beginners see no visible 'slow, low-risk beginner lane' between a savings account and 'real investing'; investing looks all-or-nothing and experts-only, so inherited fear wins.
- (idea) First-30s: opening line names the fear — "Most people here have never invested. Nothing you do tonight moves real money."
- (idea) First-30s: up-front promise — no account, no card, no commitment for the first 10 minutes.
- (idea) First-30s: pool visual (shallow/middle/deep end) shown before any explanation; marks user's spot: "You're standing at the edge. That's fine."
- (idea) First-30s: no sign-up wall; ask for email only once they have something worth saving.
- (idea) Placement Q: "Where does your money live right now?" (checking / savings / HYSA / not sure).
- (idea) Placement Q: "When might you need this money?" (<1y / 1-5y / long time) — sorts cash vs investable money better than a risk quiz.
- (idea) Placement Q: "What worries you most?" (losing it / not understanding / scams / looking dumb) — answer sets the tone of everything after.
- (idea) Placement Q: "Has anyone close to you had a bad experience investing?" — names the relative's story; app reframes it as the deep end, with another way in.
- (idea) Placement Q: "Do you have a 401(k)/work retirement plan?" — many are already investors without knowing; instant confidence boost.
- (idea) Reveal lane: ladder of real on-ramps — HYSA → T-bills/CDs → 401(k) match → Roth IRA → broad index fund, each labeled by actual scariness vs how scary it sounds.
- (idea) Reveal lane: "you might already be here" check — if they have a 401(k), show what they already own in plain English.
- (idea) Reveal lane: comparison slider for their $5,000 over 10 years — regular savings vs HYSA vs broad index fund, showing ups and downs honestly, not just averages.
- (idea) Reveal lane: one-line explainer of what they'd own — "a tiny slice of 500 big companies, not a bet on one."
- (idea) Reveal lane: explicitly separate the deep end (single stocks, options, crypto, day trading = what the relative did) from the lane, which needs none of them.
- (idea) Practice: simulator with pretend $5,000 — "buy" a broad index fund in two taps.
- (idea) Practice: time-machine button replays a real bad year (2008, 2020) on the pretend portfolio then fast-forwards the recovery — feeling a drop safely is the most valuable rep.
- (idea) Practice: "what would you do?" moment — pretend $5,000 drops to $4,100: sell/hold/buy more? Then show historical outcome of each choice.
- (idea) Practice: first-trade rehearsal — walkthrough of a realistic brokerage order screen with fake data so the real one feels familiar.
- (idea) Practice: every action paired with a 20-second micro-lesson on the one word just encountered (ETF, expense ratio).
- (idea) Tiny first step: two buckets — emergency fund stays safe vs "money you won't touch for 5+ years"; not having to invest all $5,000 removes pressure.
- (idea) Tiny first step: toe-in-the-water amount ($50/$100) framed as tuition for learning, not a bet.
- (idea) Tiny first step: "do nothing yet" path still counts as progress — moving savings to a HYSA is step one.
- (idea) Tiny first step: checklist with one box pre-checked ("Opened the app and learned what an index fund is") for momentum.
- (idea) Trust: "why you can trust this" corner — plainly states how the app makes money and that it never holds their funds.
- (idea) Trust: scam-spotting card (guaranteed returns, urgency, DMs from strangers) — fear of scams is lumped in with fear of loss.
- (idea) Trust: stories from people like them ("I was 29 with $6k in savings and terrified"), not finance influencers.
- (idea) Trust: respected "I'm not ready" button — saves progress, sends one gentle reminder a week later.
- (idea) Leave-behind: one-page summary to download/email — bucket split, position in the lane, next single step.
- (idea) Leave-behind: clear dated next step ("This week, open a HYSA" / "Next month, try $100 in an index fund").
- (idea) Leave-behind: pretend portfolio persists so returning to see how it did is a reason to come back.
- (insight) Definition agreed: 'beginner lane' = series of small safe steps from savings to understood investing; app can REVEAL existing on-ramps and CREATE a practice shallow end.
